Pick the look for the new createMPLS website

Below are early theme concepts for the new site. Each one is a working homepage mockup you can scroll, so open each one, scroll all the way down, and try it on your phone too. The motion is part of the idea. We're choosing a direction (the brand world, tone and feel), not final wording or photos.

Heads up: copy is draft. Photos mix real createMPLS program photos from past years with placeholder stock images (some watermarked) that we'll license or replace with our own.
Concept 1

Top Billing

“Every kid gets top billing.”

Every createMPLS program ends in a show: a robot tournament, a listening party, a gallery walk. So the brand is a Minneapolis theater marquee where the kids are the headliners. Tickets say "Admission $0," and the logo's pointer means "right here, at your school."

Why it works
It's the most ownable and the most local. No youth STEM group looks like this, and it carries into events, social posts, merch and signage.
Watch out for
Night-time palette, so keep it warm and family-friendly. On phones, the kids need to show up on the first screen.

Concept 2

Shop Zine

“We bring the workbench. Kids make the rest.”

The site is a hand-made zine pasted up on the workbench: tape, marker notes, and sticker cutouts of real kids and the things they build. The cover price is "$0 for students & families."

Why it works
The most joyful and kid-made of the set. It looks made, not bought, which fits hands-on learning.
Watch out for
Can get noisy. The colorized hero photo hides the kids' faces, and the crossed-out headline is hard to read.

Concept 3

Build Set 612

“Some assembly required. Every part included.”

The site reads like a step-by-step building instruction booklet (612 is the Minneapolis area code). Kids do the building; createMPLS brings every other part: tools, mentors, curriculum, and the trip to their school.

Why it works
Explains the model instantly and with humor: "1x curious kid, 1x mentor, 0x cost."
Watch out for
The toy bricks read as LEGO, which is a trademark risk and skews young for teens. It would need original artwork.

Concept 4

Circuit City

“Kids light up this city. Plug in your school.”

Minneapolis drawn as a glowing circuit board. The Mississippi is the main power line and each school is a node that lights up as you scroll.

Why it works
Strong scroll moments and a clear 'we connect sites across the city' story.
Watch out for
It's the most expected STEM look, and the dark, techy feel may not feel inviting to younger kids' families.

Concept 5

Working Drawings

“What would you create?”

Every section starts as a blueprint sketch and 'prints' into a full-color photo as you scroll, the same idea-build-test-revise loop kids follow in class.

Why it works
A smart metaphor that acts out the name "create."
Watch out for
The sketch effect makes kids' faces look muddy, and every photo would need a hand-drawn twin.
